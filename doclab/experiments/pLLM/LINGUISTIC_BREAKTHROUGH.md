## How It Works

### Sentences as Trajectories

Your insight was perfect: sentences are LINES through semantic space, not character soup.

Each point along the trajectory generates one token:
- Start at (m₀, λ₀)
- Evolve through Pirouette dynamics
- At each step, current basin + state → next word
- Result: frankenstein speech with stable structure

### Three Linguistic Basins

**Teal Basin**: Noun-Verb coupling (syntactic structure)
- Focuses on action: "city thinks", "sun walks"
- Active, concrete language
- Subject-predicate relationships

**Gold Basin**: Adjective-Object pairing (descriptive coherence)
- Focuses on qualities: "dark mountain", "young car"
- Nuanced, descriptive language
- Attribute-entity relationships

**Red Basin**: Context-Subject binding (semantic grounding)
- Focuses on setting: "in the morning", "near the house"
- Scene-setting, contextual
- Space-time relationships

### Word Selection

Each word is chosen by:
1. Current basin (which linguistic force dominates)
2. Coherence σ = 2m (coupling strength)
3. Coupling λ (oscillation preference)
4. Trajectory angle θ (directional bias)

The geometry DETERMINES the word choice. Not probabilistically - deterministically.

## Why It's Not Perfect Yet

### Current Limitations

1. **Small vocabulary** (15 nouns, 13 verbs, 15 adjectives)
   - Need larger lexicon
   - More word categories (adverbs, prepositions, etc.)

2. **Simple mapping** (angle → index)
   - Crude selection function
   - Need learned word embeddings

3. **No semantic coherence** ("city thinks")
   - Words grammatically compatible but semantically odd
   - Need semantic constraints

4. **Fixed structure** (subject-verb-object-context)
   - Always 4-part sentences
   - Need flexible syntax trees

**Current**: Geometry → word choice (deterministic but clunky)
