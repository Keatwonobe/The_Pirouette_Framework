## Law
The scattering process $e(p) + \gamma(k) \to e(p') + \gamma(k')$ is governed at tree level by the quantum electrodynamics Lagrangian $\mathcal{L}=-\tfrac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}+\bar{\Psi}(i\gamma^\mu D_\mu-m)\Psi$, where $D_\mu = \partial_\mu + iqA_\mu$. The Lorentz-invariant amplitude $\mathcal{M}$ is the sum of the $s$-channel and $u$-channel diagrams:
\[
i\mathcal{M} = (-iq)^2 \bar{u}(p') \left[ \varepsilon'_\mu \gamma^\mu \frac{i(\not p+\not k+m)}{(p+k)^2-m^2} \gamma^\nu \varepsilon_\nu + \varepsilon_\nu \gamma^\nu \frac{i(\not p-\not k'+m)}{(p-k')^2-m^2} \gamma^\mu \varepsilon'_\mu \right] u(p).
\]
Defining Mandelstam variables $s=(p+k)^2$, $u=(p-k')^2$, $t=(k-k')^2$ subject to $s+u+t=2m^2$, and averaging over initial and summing over final spins and polarizations, the squared amplitude becomes:
\[
|\overline{\mathcal{M}}|^2 = \frac{1}{4} \sum_{\text{spins, pol.}} |\mathcal{M}|^2 = 2q^4 \left[ \frac{s-m^2}{u-m^2} + \frac{u-m^2}{s-m^2} + 2m^2 \left( \frac{1}{s-m^2} + \frac{1}{u-m^2} \right) - 2m^4 \left( \frac{1}{s-m^2} + \frac{1}{u-m^2} \right)^2 \right].
\]
In the electron rest frame (ERF), where $p^\mu=(m,\mathbf{0})$, these simplify using $s-m^2 = 2m\omega$ and $u-m^2 = -2m\omega'$. Energy-momentum conservation dictates the Compton shift:
\[
\frac{\omega'}{\omega} = \left[ 1 + \frac{\omega}{m}(1-\cos\theta) \right]^{-1}.
\]
The differential cross section is given by $\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{1}{64\pi^2 m^2} \frac{\omega'}{\omega} |\overline{\mathcal{M}}|^2$. Substituting the ERF invariants yields the Klein-Nishina formula, a falsifiable prediction for the angular distribution of scattered photons:
\[
\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{\alpha^2}{2m^2}\left(\frac{\omega'}{\omega}\right)^2 \left(\frac{\omega'}{\omega} + \frac{\omega}{\omega'} - \sin^2\theta\right), \quad \text{where } \alpha = \frac{q^2}{4\pi}.
\]
This expression's empirical verification validates the predictive power of the QED vertex and propagator structure. Its failure beyond predicted higher-order corrections would falsify the theory's foundational axioms.

## Philosophy
The empirical success of a formal mathematical structure does not prove it to be an ontology of ultimate reality. Rather, it reveals that the symmetries and conserved quantities of that structure are the robust, invariant forms that survive a projection from a deeper, potentially inaccessible substrate into the realm of observation. The fact that the Klein-Nishina cross section can be derived identically from a fundamental substrate via a mapping ($\Sigma$) or from an axiomatic field theory demonstrates that physical law is not necessarily a description of the substrate itself, but a description of the rigid, conserved geometry of its shadow. We do not measure the thing-in-itself; we measure the immutable algebra of its appearance.
