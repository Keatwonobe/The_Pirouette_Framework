## Law
The fundamental principle is that the inverse dimensionless gauge couplings, $\alpha_i^{-1}$, are proportional to participation constants $K_i$ derived from a φ-space lattice binding mechanism. A single closure constant, $c_{norm}^*$, unifies the sectors.

The participation constants $K_i$ for the gauge groups $G_i \in \{U(1), SU(2), SU(3)\}$ are determined by minimizing a cross-entropy loss function $\mathcal{L}_{CE}$ between the predicted couplings $\alpha_i(\mathbf{K})$ and the experimentally measured couplings $\alpha_i^{exp}$ at the reference energy scale $\Lambda = M_Z$:
$$
\{\hat{K}_{U1}, \hat{K}_{SU2}, \hat{K}_{SU3}\} = \underset{\mathbf{K}}{\arg\min} \ \mathcal{L}_{CE}(\alpha_i(\mathbf{K}) || \alpha_i^{exp})
$$
The empirically derived constants from this closure are:
- $K_{U1} = 2.624811$
- $K_{SU2} = 1.877661$
- $K_{SU3} = 1.047475$

From these constants and a self-consistently derived closure constant $c_{norm}^* = 0.1177$, the Standard Model couplings are reproduced. The relationship is posited as a geometric proportionality, $\alpha_i^{-1}(\Lambda) \propto f(K_i, c_{norm}^*)$. This procedure yields the following predictions at $M_Z \approx 91.2$ GeV:
- Weak mixing angle: $\sin^2\theta_W(M_Z) = 0.230975$
- Strong coupling constant: $\alpha_s(M_Z) = 0.118402$
- Fine-structure constant: $\alpha_{em}(M_Z) = 0.007815248$

**Falsifiable Criteria:**
1.  The model is falsified if the predicted ratios $(K_{U1}:K_{SU2}:K_{SU3})$ fail to hold for other phenomena described by the framework or require significant tuning.
2.  The predicted energy-scale dependence of couplings ($\Lambda_B$ progression) must match future experimental results from high-energy colliders. For example, at $\Lambda = 1$ TeV, the model predicts $\sin^2\theta_W = 0.22291$ and $\alpha_s = 0.15188$.
3.  Discovery of phenomena that violate the geometrical constraints imposed by the φ-space lattice would invalidate the framework.

## Philosophy
The fundamental constants of nature are not arbitrary, contingent values to be measured, but are necessary harmonic ratios determined by the fixed geometry of an underlying substrate. The apparent complexity and specificity of the universe's physical laws are an emergent consequence of a singular, geometrically stable structure, shifting the foundation of physics from empirical contingency to rational necessity.
