## Law
The Yukawa-like coefficient for a fermion family `i`, denoted \( y_{\Gamma,i} \), is not a fundamental constant but a dimensionless overlap integral defined by the fermion's spatial profile \( \psi_i(x) \) interacting with a background field \( \Gamma(x) \):
\[
y_{\Gamma,i} \equiv \int d^3x\; \bar\psi_i(x)\, \psi_i(x)\, \frac{\partial_\mu \Gamma(x)}{\Lambda_\Gamma}\, n^\mu
\]
where \( \Lambda_\Gamma \) is a normalization scale with dimensions of mass, \( [ \Lambda_\Gamma ] = M \), and \( n^\mu \) is a directional vector.

The dimensionless nature of \( y_{\Gamma,i} \) is derived formally:
\[
[y_{\Gamma,i}] = [d^3x] \cdot [\bar\psi_i \psi_i] \cdot [\partial_\mu \Gamma] \cdot [\Lambda_\Gamma]^{-1} = M^{-3} \cdot M^3 \cdot M^1 \cdot M^{-1} = M^0
\]
The effective physical mass \( m_i \) emerges by coupling this dimensionless geometric factor to the dimensionful parameters of the background field:
\[
m_i = y_{\Gamma,i} \, \Gamma_0 \, \Lambda_\Gamma
\]
where \( \Gamma_0 \) is the dimensionless vacuum expectation value of the field.

This framework is falsifiable. It posits that for any two fermion families `i` and `j`, the ratio of their masses is strictly determined by the ratio of their geometric overlap integrals:
\[
\frac{m_i}{m_j} = \frac{y_{\Gamma,i}}{y_{\Gamma,j}}
\]
An experimental determination of fermion spatial profiles \( \psi_i, \psi_j \) that yields an integral ratio \( y_{\Gamma,i} / y_{\Gamma,j} \) inconsistent with the measured mass ratio \( m_i / m_j \) would invalidate this principle.

## Philosophy
The universe’s fundamental constants are not arbitrary, irreducible axioms but are instead emergent geometric consequences of an entity's relationship with its substrate. A particle's identity—in this case, its mass—is not an intrinsic, self-contained property but a measure of its contextual embedding and interaction within a larger, structured reality. The perceived randomness of physical parameters is a projection of a deeper, unperceived geometric order.

## Art
