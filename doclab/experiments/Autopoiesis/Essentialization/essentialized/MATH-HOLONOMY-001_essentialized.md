## Law
The composite Berry connection 1-form for a Ki-defect spinor is defined as:
\[ \mathcal{A} \equiv (\partial_\mu \theta - q A_\mu)\,dx^\mu \]
where \(\theta\) is the internal phase of the spinor, \(A_\mu\) is the ambient U(1) gauge potential, and \(q\) is the coupling constant.

The holonomy \(\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}\) for parallel transport of the spinor around a closed loop \(\mathcal{C}\) in configuration space is the phase acquired, given by the loop integral of the connection:
\[ |\psi\rangle \mapsto e^{i\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}} |\psi\rangle, \quad \text{where} \quad \gamma_{\mathcal{C}} = \oint_{\mathcal{C}} \mathcal{A} \]
The physical requirement that the state vector \(|\psi\rangle\) be single-valued imposes a quantization condition on the holonomy:
\[ e^{i\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}} = 1 \implies \gamma_{\mathcal{C}} = 2\pi n, \quad n \in \mathbb{Z} \]
Substituting the definition of \(\mathcal{A}\) yields the quantization of the coupling constant \(q\):
\[ \oint_{\mathcal{C}} (\partial_\mu \theta - q A_\mu)\,dx^\mu = 2\pi n \]
\[ 2\pi w - q \Phi_A(\mathcal{C}) = 2\pi n \implies q = \frac{2\pi(w-n)}{\Phi_A(\mathcal{C})} \]
where \(w \equiv \frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{\mathcal{C}} \partial_\mu \theta \,dx^\mu\) is the integer winding number of the spinor's internal phase and \(\Phi_A(\mathcal{C}) \equiv \oint_{\mathcal{C}} A_\mu dx^\mu\) is the magnetic flux through the loop.

The spin-½ axiom is satisfied by textures where a 360° rotation (\(\Delta\varphi = 2\pi\)) corresponds to a holonomy of \(\pi\). For an explicit texture \(Ki(r, \vartheta, \varphi) \propto e^{i\varphi/2}\), the intrinsic winding component gives:
\[ \gamma_{\mathcal{C}} \supset \int_0^{2\pi} \partial_\varphi(\varphi/2) \, d\varphi = \pi \]
This implies \(e^{i\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}} = -1\) for a single rotation (360°), and \(e^{i\gamma_{2\mathcal{C}}} = 1\) for a double rotation (720°), thereby deriving spinor behavior from the holonomy principle.

Falsifiable Criteria:
1.  **Homology Invariance:** Two homologous loops \(\mathcal{C}_1, \mathcal{C}_2\) must yield \(\gamma_{\mathcal{C}_1} \equiv \gamma_{\mathcal{C}_2} \pmod{2\pi}\).
2.  **Aharonov–Bohm Correspondence:** An external flux \(\Phi_A\) must produce a phase shift \(\Delta\phi = q\Phi_A/\hbar\), with \(q\) determined by the quantization rule above.
3.  **Existence of Spin-½ Loops:** Loops must exist for which the holonomy \(\Phi(\gamma) = e^{i\pi} = -1\) while \(\Phi(\gamma^2) = e^{i2\pi} = +1\).

## Philosophy
Fundamental properties of matter, such as electric charge and spin, are not intrinsic, axiomatic labels attached to particles. They are the necessary and quantized consequences of a deeper, more primitive principle: the topological integrity of the universe. For the world to be self-consistent—for a state to return to itself after a closed journey—the geometry of its underlying phase space dictates that the relational couplings (charge) and rotational behaviors (spin) must crystallize into discrete, integer or half-integer values. The constants of nature are not arbitrary settings, but the calculable toll that reality exacts for a path to close upon itself.

## Art
A particle's charge is the integer toll paid for a journey around a tear in spacetime. Its spin is the topological law that to truly return home, one must sometimes turn around twice.
