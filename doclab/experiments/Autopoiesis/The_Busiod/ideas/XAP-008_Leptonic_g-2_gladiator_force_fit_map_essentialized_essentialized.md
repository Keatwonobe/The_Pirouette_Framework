## The Mechanism (Physics of the Deal)
*   **Universal Archetype:** Constrained Parameter Space Cartography.
*   **The Inefficiency:** The modern market is a vast, high-dimensional "parameter space" of assets (e.g., every used car for sale). Buyers possess complex, multi-dimensional requirements (e.g., price, mileage, color, location, condition), which are analogous to the tight "experimental bounds" of a physics experiment. However, the market's search tools are primitive, low-dimensional filters (like sorting by price), forcing the buyer to expend enormous energy and time ($\Gamma$, labor) to manually sift through noise. The market fails to efficiently map the "allowed surface" of viable assets for a given set of constraints.
*   **The Pivot:** We exploit this by treating the search as a cartographic exercise, not a transactional one. We create a mechanism that ingests the entire parameter space of a market and applies complex, multi-variable constraint systems (the "system of inequalities") to chart the small, valuable surface of viable options. We sell the map, not the territory. The value is created structurally ($K_i$) by our superior mapping engine, which defines truth by what it is not—by filtering out the entire "continent of possibility" that fails the buyer's specific constraints.

## Tier 1: The Probe ($10)
*   **Concept:** Manual Constraint Application. We act as a human computational engine to validate that a market exists for pre-filtered, high-relevance information.
*   **Execution:**
    1.  Identify a niche community with complex, multi-point purchasing requirements (e.g., collectors of vintage watches, specific camera lenses, or rare books).
    2.  Use the $10 to run a hyper-targeted ad or post in a dedicated forum with a clear offer: "Stop searching. For $5, I will manually build a complete report of every item in North America that meets your exact N-point criteria (e.g., model, year, condition, price, seller rating)."
    3.  When a request is received, we manually perform the high-dimensional search, applying the customer's "constraints" to the "parameter space" of all relevant marketplaces.
    4.  Deliver the resulting "map" of viable items as a simple report.
*   **The Test:** The premise is falsified if we cannot sell at least two reports within 48 hours. This would prove that the "search cost" inefficiency ($\Gamma$) is not high enough for buyers to pay a premium to eliminate it.

## Tier 2: The Loop ($100)
*   **Concept:** The Automated Cartographer. This is a self-sustaining system that continuously maps a single market's parameter space and sells access to its filtering capabilities.
*   **Automation:**
    1.  A script (e.g., Python with Scrapy) runs continuously, scraping the entire parameter space of the niche validated in the Probe (e.g., all listings from eBay, Chrono24, and relevant forums).
    2.  This data is stored in a structured database, creating a live model of all possibilities.
    3.  A simple web interface allows users to input their complex "system of inequalities" (e.g., Brand=X, Year<1980, Condition>8/10, Price<$2000).
    4.  Upon payment, the system queries the database against these constraints and instantly generates and delivers the "map" of all matching assets.
*   **Value Capture:** A small fee ($5-10) is charged per generated report. The system generates value passively ($K_i$) by constantly maintaining the map and running the filter. It operates 24/7, turning a high-labor ($\Gamma$) search process into an instant, on-demand information product.

## Tier 3: The Engine ($1000)
*   **Concept:** The Generalized Constraint Manifold. This scales the Loop into a universal, market-agnostic platform for minimizing transactional "action" (the sum of search cost and opportunity cost).
*   **The Moat:** Standard marketplaces (eBay, Craigslist) compete by maximizing the size of their parameter space (inventory). They force the user to provide the computational energy ($\Gamma$) to navigate it. Our moat is the opposite: we are the **computational filter itself**. Our system is architected to solve high-dimensional constraint problems, a task for which standard search-and-list platforms are fundamentally unsuited.
    *   **Generalization:** We use the $1000 to build a platform where users can define any parameter space (by providing data sources/APIs) and build their own complex constraint-solvers.
    *   **Lagrangian Minimization:** The engine doesn't just find matches; it finds the most efficient path. It can run in reverse, allowing sellers to see the precise constraint-sets of anonymous buyers who are searching for their exact item. This minimizes total system friction by connecting supply and demand on a multi-dimensional "viability surface," making the old method of keyword searching obsolete. We don't just find the needle; we dissolve the haystack.

## Implementation Notes
*   **Tools:** Python (Scrapy, Pandas) for data ingestion/scraping. A simple database (PostgreSQL, SQLite). A web framework (Flask/Django) for the interface. Stripe API for payments.
*   **Risk:** The primary risk is data accessibility. The system's value is proportional to the comprehensiveness of the "parameter space" it can map. If marketplaces increasingly block scrapers or gatekeep their APIs, the engine's effectiveness is reduced. This can be mitigated by focusing on niches with open data or community-based marketplaces.
