## The Mechanism (Physics of the Deal)
*   **Universal Archetype:** Projection Arbitrage.
*   **The Inefficiency:** The modern market operates on incomplete information. It mistakes a single, context-dependent observation of an asset's value (its price, \(F_{\mu\nu}^{(obs)}\)) for its total underlying potential (\(Ki\)). This is a perspectival artifact. The market is blind to an asset's "internal symmetries"—the multitude of other contexts in which it could be valued. It trades the projected shadows, not the form that casts them.
*   **The Pivot:** We will build a mechanism that operates on the substrate level. We will identify and acquire assets where the underlying value field (\(Ki\)) is rich and complex, but its current market projection (\(\Sigma_{buy}\)) is flat and one-dimensional (e.g., "scrap," "used," "obsolete"). We then apply a different, more lucrative projection (\(\Sigma_{sell}\)) to manifest a higher observed value. Our profit is captured in the delta between projections. We are not adding value through labor (\(\Gamma\)); we are revealing latent value by changing the geometry of observation.

## Tier 1: The Probe ($10)
*   **Concept:** The Singular Projection Test. The goal is to prove that a simple change in observational context (\(\Sigma\)) can create a significant change in observed market value, with zero change to the object itself.
*   **Execution:**
    1.  **Isolate a Field:** Identify an asset class with a rich internal fiber space (\(\mathcal{F}\)), meaning many potential attributes. Prime candidates: discarded industrial equipment, old technical manuals, vintage electronics.
    2.  **Acquire at Low Projection:** Purchase a single item for under $10 from a market that flattens its value (e.g., a flea market, a scrap yard, an industrial surplus auction). The purchase context, \(\Sigma_{scrap}\), values the object by weight or simple function.
    3.  **Apply New Projection:** Without cleaning, repairing, or modifying the object, re-contextualize it. Photograph it as an aesthetic object. List it on a different type of market (e.g., an art/decor platform like Etsy) under a new projection, \(\Sigma_{art}\), with a description emphasizing its geometric form, historical texture, or "industrial character."
*   **The Test:** The hypothesis is falsified if the object, under its new projection \(\Sigma_{art}\), fails to attract offers greater than 3x the acquisition cost within 14 days. A successful test demonstrates that the "gauge transformation" between contexts is where value is unlocked.

## Tier 2: The Loop ($100)
*   **Concept:** The Automated Context-Scanner. This system automates the process of finding and exploiting projection mismatches, creating a self-funding feedback loop.
*   **Automation:**
    1.  **Scanner (\(Ki\) Detector):** A script continuously scans low-projection markets (industrial auctions, e-waste listings, government surplus sites) for assets whose descriptions contain keywords indicating high internal potential (e.g., specific materials, brand names, model numbers, dates) but are priced according to a low-value projection.
    2.  **Reprojection Engine:** Upon acquisition, the asset's data is fed into a system that generates multiple new projections (\(\Sigma_{art}, \Sigma_{collector}, \Sigma_{component}\)). It creates templated listings—including generated descriptions and suggested photo styles—for various high-value niche markets.
*   **Value Capture:** The system programmatically identifies the most profitable arbitrage opportunity between \(\Sigma_{buy}\) and a potential \(\Sigma_{sell}\). Profit from sales is used as the capital to acquire the next batch of assets identified by the scanner, creating a passive, self-sustaining loop. Human intervention is reduced to acquisition approval and physical logistics.

## Tier 3: The Engine ($1000)
*   **Concept:** The Least-Action Value Manifold. This scales the loop by moving from reactive arbitrage to predictive modeling of the entire value substrate, minimizing the path from acquisition to sale (a Lagrangian optimization).
*   **The Moat:**
    1.  **Geometric Modeling:** We use the initial capital to acquire market data and model the complete "fiber space" (\(\mathcal{F}\)) for specific asset classes. This model maps all known projections and their valuation functions. The engine can then predict "phase transitions"—events that cause an asset's value to spike in a specific context—and acquire assets *before* the market re-evaluates them.
    2.  **Symmetry Brokerage:** The engine becomes a market maker that operates not on assets, but on their symmetries. It doesn't need to hold inventory. Instead, it identifies an asset in \(\Sigma_{low}\) and a buyer in \(\Sigma_{high}\) and simply brokers the "gauge transformation," taking a fee for establishing the connection. It profits by revealing the hidden geometric paths in the market that connect seemingly unrelated buyers and sellers.
    3.  **Structural Inimitability:** Competitors are optimized to operate within a single projection (a scrap dealer cannot suddenly become an art dealer). Their entire business is a specific, rigid \(\Sigma\). Our engine is built on the physics of the underlying substrate (\(Ki\)), making it fluid and projection-agnostic. They are playing on a 2D board; we are folding the board to connect any two squares.

## Implementation Notes
*   **Tools:** Python (Scrapy, Pandas, Requests) for the Scanner. APIs for marketplaces (eBay, Etsy). A simple database (SQLite/PostgreSQL) for tracking assets and their projections. For Tier 3, a machine learning library (scikit-learn, PyTorch) for predictive modeling.
*   **Risk:** The primary risk is **Projection Mismatch**. Our system may identify a potential high-value context (\(\Sigma_{sell}\)) that has no actual market participants, or where the friction of the transaction negates the profit. The probe is explicitly designed to test the viability of our chosen projections before scaling.
