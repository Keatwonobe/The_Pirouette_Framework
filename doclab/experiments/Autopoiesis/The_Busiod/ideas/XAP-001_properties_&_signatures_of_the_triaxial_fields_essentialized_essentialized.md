## The Mechanism (Physics of the Deal)
* **Universal Archetype:** Cohesion Arbitrage
* **The Inefficiency:** The modern market prices assets using a "gravitational" model, observing only weak, long-range forces like brand recognition and commoditized supply/demand. It is functionally blind to the powerful, short-range "strong force" (the Gladiator Force, Γ) that creates cohesive value between components. This is the unpriced potential energy (`1 + αe^{-r/λ}`) that exists when assets are "close" in a functional, conceptual, or geographic space.
* **The Pivot:** We will exploit this inefficiency by building a system that measures this cohesive potential. It will identify undervalued, disparate components ("quarks") and calculate the path of least action to bind them into high-value, stable systems ("baryons"), capturing the immense value delta the market cannot see.

## Tier 1: The Probe ($10)
* **Concept:** A micro-experiment to validate the existence of unpriced "Cohesive Potential" between two low-value assets.
* **Execution:**
    1.  Select a narrow, tangible domain (e.g., vintage camera equipment, specialized electronic components, out-of-print book sets).
    2.  Identify a "baryon" (a functional whole, e.g., a specific working vintage camera) and its constituent "quarks" (e.g., the camera body and its specific, rare lens, sold separately by different sellers).
    3.  Use the $10 budget to acquire two or more "quarks" whose combined market price is a fraction of the whole "baryon's" value.
    4.  Perform the "binding action" (e.g., physically connect the lens to the body, clean the unit, and photograph it as a complete kit).
    5.  List the unified asset for sale at its proper "baryonic" valuation.
* **The Test:** The hypothesis is falsified if the final sale price is not at least double the total acquisition cost (`P(M) < 2 * P(m1+m2)`). A result less than this indicates we have only captured linear labor value, not the predicted non-linear value from realizing cohesive potential.

## Tier 2: The Loop ($100)
* **Concept:** An automated discovery system that perpetually scans for and flags Cohesion Arbitrage opportunities, creating a self-sustaining value feedback loop.
* **Automation:** A software agent continuously monitors low-value marketplaces (e.g., government surplus auctions, local classifieds, eBay's "for parts/not working" category). It uses a semantic model to find items that are "close" in the value space (`r < λ`)—for example, an industrial server chassis in one listing and the specific RAID controller it requires in another. When a high-potential pair (`α`) is found, it is flagged for acquisition.
* **Value Capture:** A $100 capital float is used to acquire the component sets flagged by the agent. A human operator performs the physical "binding." The assembled, higher-value item is sold, and the profit is returned to the float. The value is generated passively by the intelligence of the search structure (`K_i`), not the active labor of searching (`Γ`).

## Tier 3: The Engine ($1000)
* **Concept:** Lagrangian Value-Path Optimization. This system scales the Loop from simple pairs to complex, multi-component systems by modeling the entire landscape of undervalued assets as a field of potential.
* **The Moat:** Standard commerce operates on Newtonian principles—building linear supply chains to manufacture known objects. Our Engine operates on quantum principles. It does not manufacture; it "collapses the wavefunction" of the scrap heap, finding the path of least action (lowest cost and effort) to assemble the most valuable possible wholes from the economy's discarded fragments. Our moat is this proprietary physics model of value itself. We can synthesize value in domains where competitors see only entropy and waste, because they are observing the shadow (price), while we are manipulating the titan (potential).

## Implementation Notes
* **Tools:** Python (Scrapy, Pandas), a vector database (e.g., Pinecone) for semantic proximity search, marketplace APIs, basic hand tools for physical assembly.
* **Risk:** The primary risk is model failure. If our ontology for measuring "conceptual proximity" (`λ`) and "cohesive strength" (`α`) is inaccurate, the system will acquire assets that cannot be profitably combined, leading to capital loss. The Probe is designed to test this core assumption cheaply.
