## The Mechanism (Physics of the Deal)
*   **Universal Archetype:** Decision Singularity Resolution
*   **The Inefficiency:** The modern market operates under the false assumption that participant indecision or inaction is a stable state of low interest. The Pirouette framework reveals this is often "critical slowing"—a predictable, pre-collapse state of extreme cognitive load (Γ → Γ_c). In this state, a system (a seller, a company, a department) becomes brittle and hypersensitive to low-energy inputs. The market is blind to this physics, creating vast pockets of potential energy (V) trapped in decision paralysis.
*   **The Pivot:** Instead of adding to the cognitive load (Γ) through negotiation or feature comparison, we identify systems exhibiting critical slowing (τ_P divergence) and introduce a single, simple, low-entropy "resolution vector." This catalyzes the inevitable collapse of the decision-state into our desired outcome, allowing us to acquire assets (information, inventory, opportunities) at a cost basis reflecting their cognitive weight, not their intrinsic value. We are arbitraging decision-energy.

## Tier 1: The Probe ($10)
*   **Concept:** Micro-Acquisition via Load Collapse. We will prove that a market participant exhibiting measurable "slowing" will accept a suboptimal offer if it represents a dramatic reduction in their cognitive load.
*   **Execution:**
    1.  Identify a target on a high-friction public marketplace (e.g., Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist).
    2.  The target must exhibit at least two signs of "critical slowing":
        *   Multiple relistings of the same item over weeks.
        *   Decreasing price in small, frustrated increments.
        *   Description contains language of fatigue ("firm price," "will not respond to...").
    3.  Make a clean, simple, cash offer slightly below the current asking price. The offer's primary feature is its simplicity: "I will be there in 1 hour with cash. No questions. Final offer." This is the resolution vector. The $10 represents the operating capital for this first acquisition.
*   **The Test:** The hypothesis is that the seller's system, brittle from high Γ, will collapse along the path of least cognitive resistance. **The Probe is falsified if fewer than 60% (3 out of 5) of targeted, pre-screened sellers accept the simple, immediate offer.** This would indicate our proxies for critical slowing are incorrect, or the phenomenon is not exploitable.

## Tier 2: The Loop ($100)
*   **Concept:** Automated Criticality Scanner. This is a self-sustaining feedback loop where profit from one resolution funds the next, powered by an automated detection system. This is the "Passive" layer.
*   **Automation:** A script continuously scrapes public marketplaces, but it does not search for low prices. It searches for the *physics* of collapse. It builds a database of listings and tracks their change over time, flagging assets that exhibit high τ_P (long relisting times) and Γ→Γ_c indicators (price decay, sentiment analysis of descriptions). This system is our sensor for market inefficiency.
*   **Value Capture:** The system generates a daily list of high-potential targets. A templated "resolution vector" offer is sent. An acquisition is made. The asset is then re-listed with high clarity and low friction (a low-Γ presentation), capturing the value spread. The profit (e.g., $20-$50) is cycled back into the operating float ($100), creating an autopoietic, self-funding loop. The value is generated by the *structure* of our low-Γ system ($K_i$), not from continuous labor ($\Gamma$).

## Tier 3: The Engine ($1000)
*   **Concept:** The Cognitive Load Exchange. This scales the Loop from asset acquisition to becoming a market utility that minimizes action along a Lagrangian path (L=T-V).
*   **The Moat:** Standard businesses compete on features, price, and selection, which inherently *increases* cognitive load (Γ) for the consumer. Our Engine does the opposite. We build a two-sided platform that is fundamentally a sink for cognitive load.
    1.  **Supply-Side (High-V Liquidation):** We offer a service to individuals and businesses to liquidate entire inventories of stagnant, high-Γ assets (e.g., "We will clear your entire garage/storage unit/parts bin for a single bulk price, tomorrow."). We absorb their cognitive chaos.
    2.  **Demand-Side (Zero-Γ Curation):** We do not offer choice. We use user data and system history to present customers with a single, curated solution to their problem. We collapse the entire "shopping" process into a single "accept/reject" decision.
    Our competitive advantage is not based on market economics but on the fundamental physics of decision-making. We are not a store; we are a utility for offloading cognitive work.

## Implementation Notes
*   **Tools:**
    *   Probe: Smartphone, small cash float.
    *   Loop: Python (Scrapy, BeautifulSoup, Pandas, NLTK for sentiment analysis), SQLite.
    *   Engine: Cloud server (AWS/GCP), PostgreSQL, web framework (Django/Flask), logistics/payment APIs.
*   **Risk:** The primary risk is at the systemic level. If the underlying market becomes significantly more efficient (e.g., platforms simplify their interfaces and reduce friction), the available potential energy (V) for our arbitrage would decrease. This is a slow-moving, long-term risk. The immediate risk is misinterpreting the signals of critical slowing, which the Probe is designed to validate.
