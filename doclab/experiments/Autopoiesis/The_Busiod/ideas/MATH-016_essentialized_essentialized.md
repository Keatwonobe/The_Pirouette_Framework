## The Mechanism (Physics of the Deal)
*   **Universal Archetype:** Conserved Charge Aggregation (Q-ball Formation)
*   **The Inefficiency:** The modern market operates under the false assumption that value is maximized by increasing the rate of activity (transactions, interactions, growth velocity). In the language of the Framework, it tries to maximize the internal frequency (`ω`). However, the physical law for stable value concentration (`dQ/dω < 0`) dictates that the most stable, highest-charge (`Q`) configurations are often those with a *lower* internal frequency. The market builds inherently unstable structures that require constant energy input, while overlooking the massive potential of slow, stable, high-charge value entities.
*   **The Pivot:** We will exploit this by building systems that explicitly optimize for `Q` (a conserved quantity like verified information, trust, or provenance) by finding the path of least action—the optimal, often slower, `ω`. We will build "value batteries" that passively aggregate charge, while the rest of the market builds leaky, high-maintenance engines.

## Tier 1: The Probe ($10)
*   **Concept:** The Information Seed. This is an experiment to prove that a small, dense nucleus of a conserved charge (`Q`) can form a stable configuration in a chosen market "vacuum" (`Γ_vac`).
*   **Execution:**
    1.  Identify a domain where valuable information is fragmented, disorganized, and has a high cost of verification (a high potential `V`). Examples: Compatibility of legacy hardware components, chemical purity data for specific nootropics, provenance records for digital art.
    2.  Use the $10 to acquire and definitively verify a single, non-trivial "quantum" of this information. This could involve buying a rare manual, paying for a single database query, or performing a simple chemical test. This is our `Q`.
    3.  Publish this single piece of verified information in a permanent, public, and easily citable location (e.g., a dedicated wiki page, a public hash on a ledger, a GitHub Gist). This is the initial value concentration, `φ(r)`. The act of keeping it online is its "hum," `ω`.
*   **The Test:** The experiment is a failure if, within 60 days, this "Information Seed" receives zero organic signals of value (e.g., no external links, no search engine queries leading to it, no citations in forums). This would prove that the chosen `Q` is not conserved or valued by the market, and a Q-ball cannot form in this potential `V`.

## Tier 2: The Loop ($100)
*   **Concept:** The Autopoietic Aggregator. This creates a self-sustaining feedback loop where the existing charge (`Q`) attracts more charge, causing the Q-ball to grow.
*   **Automation:**
    1.  Invest the $100 to build a simple, automated ingestion system around the successful "Information Seed". This could be a web form with strict validation, a script that parses specific forum posts, or an API endpoint.
    2.  The core of the automation is a verification oracle. This system uses the existing trusted data (`Q`) to cross-reference and validate new, incoming data. This process models the interaction between the fields `φ` and `Γ`.
    3.  The growing reputation of the system as a source of truth lowers the activation energy for others to contribute, creating a flywheel effect. The structure begins to feed itself.
*   **Value Capture:** We do not sell the `Q` itself, as the charge must be conserved. Instead, we monetize the *gradient* around the dense information core. This is achieved by selling structured access via a premium API, offering advanced analytical "lenses" on the data, or creating a marketplace for services that rely on our verified information. The value comes from the structure (`K_i`), not from labor (`Γ`).

## Tier 3: The Engine ($1000)
*   **Concept:** The Lagrangian Optimizer. This scales the system by treating Q-ball formation as a physics problem, actively managing the system's parameters to find the path of least action for maximal charge aggregation.
*   **The Moat:** Our competitive advantage is baked into the fundamental physics.
    1.  **Counter-intuitive Optimization:** While competitors chase higher `ω` (more features, more user interactions, faster updates), we use the $1000 to build monitoring tools that track the `dQ/dω` ratio. We will deliberately *slow down* or simplify aspects of the system if our models show it will lead to a more stable, higher-charge state (`Q`). We might reduce update frequency or prune low-quality information to increase the overall density and trustworthiness.
    2.  **Structural Stability:** Our focus on the `dQ/dω < 0` stability criterion means our systems become more robust and require less energy to maintain as they grow. Competitors' systems become more fragile and expensive, as they are operating in the unstable regime of the phase space.
    3.  **Inevitability:** We are not forcing a business model onto the market; we are creating the conditions (`V` and `ω`) for value to spontaneously crystallize into a stable form (`φ`). Standard businesses cannot compete because they are fighting this tendency toward equilibrium, while we are channeling it.

## Implementation Notes
*   **Tools:** Python (Flask/FastAPI for web endpoints), a document or vector database (e.g., Chroma, Weaviate) for storing and verifying semantic information, and a simple cloud VM for hosting.
*   **Risk:** The primary risk is misidentifying a truly "conserved" quantity. If the information (`Q`) we choose to aggregate is ephemeral, easily replicated, or not sufficiently valued by the market, the potential `V` will be too shallow for a Q-ball to form, and the Probe will fail as designed.
