## The Mechanism (Physics of the Deal)
*   **Universal Archetype:** Temporal Resonance Arbitrage
*   **The Inefficiency:** Modern markets are structured around spatial locality. They price assets based on their immediate, observable context (e.g., location, current supply/demand), treating identical items in different locations as fundamentally separate. This ignores the principle of Temporal Nonlocality, which states that systems sharing a common origin or "kappa-structured history" remain phase-coherent regardless of spatial separation. The market is blind to the resonant "hum" between assets "cast in the same fire," creating pricing disparities that are not random noise but predictable phase deviations.
*   **The Pivot:** We will exploit this inefficiency by building a system that indexes assets not by their location, but by their shared history (`kappa`). By identifying these cohorts of temporally-linked assets (the "bells"), we can detect when a localized event ("the strike") affects one member and anticipate the resulting, predictable phase-shift ("the hum") in the others. This provides an information advantage, allowing us to act before the spatially-fixated market corrects the inefficiency.

## Tier 1: The Probe ($10)
*   **Concept:** To empirically validate that a localized price perturbation on one asset in a `kappa`-cohort induces a predictable, non-local effect on other cohort members faster than the general market can react.
*   **Execution:**
    1.  **Identify a Cohort:** Select a class of assets with a strong, verifiable "shared history" (`kappa`). Examples: first-edition books from the same print run, limited-edition sneakers, or NFTs from the same minting event.
    2.  **Isolate Subjects:** Locate at least two such identical items for sale on separate, minimally-connected marketplaces (e.g., "Bell A" on eBay, "Bell B" on a niche collector's forum).
    3.  **Induce Perturbation:** Create a "strike." This can be done by listing our own item far below the cohort's average price or by identifying a recent, sharp price drop of an existing listing (Bell A).
    4.  **Monitor Resonance:** Use the $10 budget for simple monitoring tools (e.g., API calls, page-refresh trackers) to track demand signals for Bell B (e.g., view counts, watcher counts, bid velocity).
*   **The Test:** The hypothesis is validated if Bell B shows a statistically significant increase in demand signals *prior* to a corresponding price correction across the broader market for that asset class. **Failure State:** If no measurable effect is observed on Bell B within 48 hours, or the effect is indistinguishable from random market noise, the probe is considered a failure, and the underlying premise is falsified.

## Tier 2: The Loop ($100)
*   **Concept:** An automated, self-sustaining system that continuously scans for `kappa`-cohorts, detects price deviations, and executes arbitrage transactions to capture the resulting value spread. This is the passive layer where the structure does the work.
*   **Automation:** A script (e.g., Python with Scrapy, marketplace APIs) will perform three functions:
    1.  **Cohort Discovery:** Continuously scan multiple online marketplaces to identify and catalog items with shared `kappa` identifiers (ISBNs, serial numbers, contract addresses), building a real-time database of resonant cohorts.
    2.  **Deviation Detection:** Monitor all assets within the database. When one "bell" is "struck" (i.e., its price drops significantly below the cohort average), it's flagged as an arbitrage opportunity.
    3.  **Execution:** The system automatically executes a transaction to capture the spread: it buys the underpriced asset.
*   **Value Capture:** The system's profit is the price differential between the purchased asset and the cohort's stable price. The initial $100 serves as the transactional float for the first automated purchases. The value is generated by the system's architectural awareness of the market's hidden temporal physics, requiring no continuous human labor ($\Gamma$).

## Tier 3: The Engine ($1000)
*   **Concept:** To scale beyond simple reactive arbitrage by using a predictive model based on the system's Hamiltonian to actively manage the price equilibrium of entire `kappa`-cohorts, transitioning from price-taker to cohort market-maker.
*   **The Moat:** Standard business logic and algorithms are fundamentally "local." They cannot perceive, let alone act upon, the temporal coherence we have identified. Our moat is not a specific tactic but a superior understanding of the market's underlying physics. While competitors see a thousand unrelated products, we see a single resonant system. The Engine will:
    1.  **Model System Dynamics:** Using the collected data, model the entire cohort as a single quantum system. A "strike" is an energy input. The model, inspired by the `H_eff` kernel, predicts how this energy will propagate through the cohort as a phase shift (price momentum).
    2.  **Minimize Action:** Instead of just buying the cheapest asset, the Engine calculates the "path of least action" (Lagrangian minimization) to guide the cohort to a new equilibrium. This may involve a complex set of actions: buying the cheap asset, placing micro-bids on others to accelerate perceived demand, and potentially shorting overpriced members on sophisticated platforms.
    3.  **Extract Systemic Value:** The Engine becomes a liquidity provider and stabilizer for these `kappa`-cohorts, extracting a small fee on every correction it facilitates. It profits not from a single arbitrage event, but from maintaining the energetic balance of the entire temporal system.

## Implementation Notes
*   **Tools:** Python (Scrapy for scraping, Pandas for data manipulation), APIs for relevant marketplaces (eBay, stock/crypto exchanges), a simple database (SQLite or PostgreSQL) for storing cohort data.
*   **Risk:** The primary risk is misidentification of `kappa`. If the "shared history" is not the dominant factor in the assets' phase coherence, the nonlocal effect will be lost in market noise. The Probe is designed specifically to mitigate this risk at minimal cost.
