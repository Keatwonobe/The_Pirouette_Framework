## The Mechanism (Physics of the Deal)
*   **Universal Archetype:** Potential Gradient Arbitrage
*   **The Inefficiency:** The modern market prices assets based on their manifest state (`\psi_\ell`) but assigns near-zero value to their "temporal pressure" (Γ) — their latent potential derived from history, context, or unrealized application. It is blind to the enormous potential energy stored in information-poor, high-inertia (`m_\ell`) assets.
*   **The Pivot:** We will exploit this by treating latent value as a physical field (Γ). The interaction Lagrangian (`\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}`) proves that value is not released by the existence of this potential, but by creating a steep **gradient** (`\partial_\mu \Gamma`) in it. Our mechanism is a system to manufacture these gradients: we find assets in low-potential states (information deserts) and move them across a sharp informational differential to a high-potential state (niche markets), forcing the Γ-field to "decay" into tangible cash (`Γ → e⁺e⁻`). The interaction is strongest for "heavy," illiquid assets (`m_\ell`), which the market is least efficient at pricing.

## Tier 1: The Probe ($10)
*   **Concept:** A single, manual test of Γ-field excitation and decay. We will acquire a "heavy" (illiquid, obscure, information-poor) asset and artificially create a maximal potential gradient (`\partial_\mu \Gamma`) around it.
*   **Execution:**
    1.  **Acquire a High-`m_\ell` Asset:** For <$10, purchase an item from a low-information environment (e.g., a miscategorized online listing, an obscure item at a thrift store). The asset must possess high latent potential (e.g., an old piece of enterprise hardware, a vintage technical manual, a craft supply with a hidden niche use).
    2.  **Excite the Γ-field:** Invest pure information (labor, $\Gamma$) to build potential. Research the item's origin, model number, technical specifications, and most valuable use cases. Document this clearly. This act is the "stressing" of the temporal-pressure background.
    3.  **Create the Gradient:** Create a high-information listing on a specialized, high-potential marketplace (e.g., a niche enthusiast forum, a targeted eBay category). The listing itself, rich with context and data, is the high-pressure node. The difference between this and its previous state is the gradient.
    4.  **Observe Decay:** The sale of the item for a significant multiple of its acquisition cost is the "decay" of the Pressuron into measurable value.
*   **The Test:** The model is falsified if, after 5 attempts, we cannot achieve an average "decay" that yields at least a 3x return on capital. This would imply the market is more efficient at pricing latent value than the physics predicts, or our method of creating a gradient is flawed.

## Tier 2: The Loop ($100)
*   **Concept:** An automated system for detecting naturally occurring potential gradients. This is the "passive" layer, where the structure ($K_i$) does the work of finding opportunities.
*   **Automation:** A software agent will be built to continuously scan for high-mass, low-potential assets and flag them for decay.
    1.  **Scanner:** A script monitors low-information marketplaces (e.g., Craigslist "for free," Facebook Marketplace "OBO," government auction sites) for keywords indicating high `m_\ell` assets ("untested," "enterprise," "vintage," "parts only").
    2.  **Enricher:** When a target is found, the system uses APIs to pull identifying information (model numbers from images via OCR, technical specs from archived manuals) and cross-reference it with pricing data from high-information marketplaces. This calculates the potential `\partial_\mu \Gamma`.
    3.  **Alerter:** When a gradient is detected that exceeds a predefined threshold (e.g., potential sale price > 10x purchase price), it alerts a human operator to execute the transaction.
*   **Value Capture:** The loop captures the spread between the purchase price in the low-information environment and the sale price in the high-information environment. The $100 is used for initial operating capital and API/server costs. The value is generated by the system's ability to perceive the temporal-pressure field that is invisible to other market participants.

## Tier 3: The Engine ($1000)
*   **Concept:** A Lagrangian-optimized platform for creating and liquidating potential gradients at scale. We shift from *finding* gradients to *manufacturing* them with minimal action.
*   **The System:** This is a two-sided marketplace that institutionalizes the process of Γ-field decay.
    1.  **Supply Aggregation (Low-Potential Zone):** The platform partners with sources of high-`m_\ell` assets (e-waste recyclers, business liquidators, estate sale managers) to provide a consistent stream of low-information inventory.
    2.  **Refiner Network (Gradient Creators):** The platform curates a network of niche specialists (e.g., vintage synth repairers, military surplus experts, rare book appraisers). These are our "particle accelerators"—they provide the expert knowledge to create a steep potential gradient (`\partial_\mu \Gamma`) with maximum efficiency.
    3.  **The Engine (Lagrangian Minimizer):** The core of the platform is a matching algorithm. It analyzes incoming inventory and routes each asset to the specific Refiner who can add the most value for the least cost/time. The platform handles listing, transaction, and splits the profit from the final "decay" with the inventory supplier and the Refiner.
*   **The Moat:** Standard businesses compete on logistical efficiency for known commodities. They optimize paths for existing value. Our Engine competes on **informational efficiency for latent value**. We are not a supply chain; we are a value chain that profits from the organized collapse of ignorance. The physics of `\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}` is our moat because competitors are not even aware the field exists.

## Implementation Notes
*   **Tools:** Python (for scripting/automation), Scrapy/BeautifulSoup (for web scraping), Google Vision API / Tesseract (for OCR), various marketplace APIs (eBay, etc.), a simple database (SQLite/PostgreSQL).
*   **Risk:** The primary risk is market adaptation. If a larger player observes our model and begins to systematically price-in latent informational value, our exploitable gradients (`\partial_\mu \Gamma`) would shrink, reducing profitability. This is a risk only at the Engine scale.
