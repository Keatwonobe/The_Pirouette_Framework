## The Mechanism (Physics of the Deal)
*   **Universal Archetype:** Contextual Arbitrage Engine.
*   **The Inefficiency:** The modern market operates on brute-force aggregation, creating a single, generic "environmental geometry" ($\kappa_G$) for all assets (e.g., eBay, Amazon). This generic geometry is dissonant with the "intrinsic fuel geometry" ($\kappa_{fuel}$) of specialized or de-contextualized assets. This creates a massive chiral differential ($\Delta\kappa$), leading to a high Coherence Ratio ($\zeta \gg 1$). Sellers must apply enormous temporal pressure ($\Gamma$)—constant relisting, price cuts, marketing labor—to overcome the resulting transactional "Coulomb barrier," yielding an abysmal ratio of profit-to-effort.
*   **The Pivot:** This mechanism does not apply more force ($\Gamma$). It acts as a "geometric tuner," building a new, resonant environmental geometry ($\kappa_G'$) for each asset class. By programmatically matching an asset's intrinsic geometry to a market that values it, we dramatically reduce $\Delta\kappa$, causing the Coherence Ratio to fall below unity ($\zeta < 1$). This makes the desired transaction the path of least resistance, creating a "Stay" interaction where buyer and seller connect with near-zero friction, maximizing yield ($y$) while minimizing input power ($P_h$).

## Tier 1: The Probe ($10)
*   **Concept:** A single, manual test of resonant re-contextualization. We will demonstrate that changing an asset's environment ($\kappa_G$) is profoundly more effective than increasing the pressure ($\Gamma$) within a poor environment.
*   **Execution:**
    1.  **Identify Fuel:** Locate a single, high-$\Delta\kappa$ asset on a generic platform (e.g., an "untested" piece of vintage scientific equipment listed for scrap value on eBay).
    2.  **Acquire:** Purchase the asset using the $10 budget.
    3.  **Sculpt Geometry:** Instead of relisting on a generic platform, identify the specific, context-rich environment (e.g., a university research forum, a specialized hobbyist mailing list) where the asset's intrinsic value is understood. This is its "characteristic frequency," $\omega_c$.
    4.  **Initiate Catalysis:** Present the asset to this niche audience, providing the necessary context.
*   **The Test:** The physics is falsified if the re-contextualization fails to create value. The Probe fails if the asset does not attract qualified offers of at least 5x the acquisition cost within 14 days from the targeted niche audience. This would be a direct measurement of $\frac{\partial y}{\partial \zeta} = 0$, proving the theory invalid.

## Tier 2: The Loop ($100)
*   **Concept:** An automated system that continuously scans for high-$\Delta\kappa$ assets and routes them to pre-configured resonant environments, creating a passive value-capture stream.
*   **Automation:**
    1.  **Scanner:** A script monitors public-facing marketplaces and liquidation feeds (the high-$\Gamma$ sources) for assets with keywords indicating a high chiral differential ("untested," "surplus," "for parts," etc., cross-referenced with a database of historically niche-valuable items).
    2.  **Mapper:** A simple knowledge graph maps identified asset classes ($\kappa_{fuel}$) to their corresponding resonant market geometries ($\kappa_G'$)—forums, subreddits, collector groups, etc.
    3.  **Router:** An automated agent posts the discovered asset into its matched environment as a "lead," or alerts an operator to perform the arbitrage manually.
*   **Value Capture:** The system generates revenue by taking a commission on successful connections (a "finder's fee"), requiring zero capital expenditure on inventory. The value is generated purely by the system's structure (its superior $\kappa_G$), not by continuous labor ($\Gamma$). The $100 budget is for initial server/API costs and seed capital for the first few automated arbitrage events.

## Tier 3: The Engine ($1000)
*   **Concept:** A multi-sided platform where niche communities can define their own "market geometries," effectively telling the Engine what kind of "fuel" they seek. The Engine then uses this information to optimize its scanning and routing, minimizing the action required to move an asset from a low-value context to a high-value one.
*   **The Moat:** Standard e-commerce cannot compete because it is built on the paradigm of brute force. It seeks to maximize yield ($y$) by increasing inventory and traffic ($\Gamma$). Our Engine is built on resonant persuasion; it seeks to maximize the *efficiency* of the system ($y/P_h$) by optimizing its geometry. This creates a powerful network effect: as more communities define their needs, the Engine's map of the potential landscape becomes more refined, making it exponentially better at finding the path of least resistance for any given asset. Competitors are playing checkers with brute force; we are applying Lagrangian mechanics to the flow of value itself.

## Implementation Notes
*   **Tools:** Python (for scripting with libraries like `BeautifulSoup`, `requests`), public marketplace APIs (eBay, etc.), a simple database (e.g., SQLite or PostgreSQL) for the Mapper, and a lightweight web framework (e.g., Flask) for The Engine.
*   **Risk:** The primary risk is a miscalculation of an asset's intrinsic geometry ($\kappa_{fuel}$). If the system incorrectly identifies an item's true context and value, it will fail to find a resonant market, and the arbitrage will fail. This is mitigated by starting with well-understood niches and iteratively improving the Mapper's accuracy.
