## Abstract
The Pirouette Plane provides a bidimensional projection of power curvature — ΔP (Power Change) versus |κ*| (Curvature Magnitude) — allowing classification of temporal systems into modal quadrants (Weaver, Gladiator, Vortex, Drifter).  
By scanning synthetic and empirical datasets, closure frequency (complete WGVD cycles) reveals a **phase diagram** distinguishing static, stochastic, and harmonic regimes.

## Findings

1. **Classification ≠ Closure**  
   Random and deterministic datasets yield strong modal classification yet minimal closure, confirming closure’s dependence on temporal curvature rather than instantaneous statistics.

2. **Temporal Dependence (Shuffle Test)**  
   Shuffling uniformly reduces closure counts to ≤ 1, proving that closure is an emergent property of temporal order, not dataset bias.

3. **Positive Control (Harmonic Systems)**  
   Sawtooth datasets exhibit persistent closure across transformations, validating the sensitivity of the WGVD cycle detector.

4. **Degenerate Controls**  
   Arithmetic and repeating sequences lock into single modes (Gladiator or Drifter) with |κ*|→0, demonstrating that closure requires **state diversity** and **sign-changing curvature**.
